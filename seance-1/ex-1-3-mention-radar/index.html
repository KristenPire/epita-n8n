    Ex 1.3 — Mention Radar 📡

    Goal: when FoodFleet's market gets mentioned online, an alert arrives in Slack.

    What you learn: polling (checking at regular intervals), filtering items with an IF node, and the worst kind of bug: the one with no error at all.

    Context: the FoodFleet founder wants to know immediately when a competitor or the market is mentioned. Not tomorrow morning. Now.

    🐛 This skeleton has ONE bug — in the 'Filter' node

    Special feature: this bug gives no red node. The workflow runs green, and sends nothing, ever. This is the most dangerous bug in automation: silent success. Good hunt.

    Step 0 — Import the skeleton

    mention-radar.json → Import from file.

    5 nodes: Schedule Trigger → Search mentions (HTTP) → Split Out → Filter (IF) → Slack.

    CP1 — Mentions arrive

    1. Open Search mentions (HTTP). It asks for the newest stories: 
      https://hn.algolia.com/api/v1/search_by_date?query=fintech&tags=story
    2. Replace fintech with a FoodFleet keyword. Keep a common word for this exercise. You need enough results to test.
    3. Execute. The Split Out node turns the hits list into separate items. Look at its OUTPUT: it is no longer ONE response containing 20 articles, it is 20 items.

    ✅ Done when: Split Out outputs several items. → CP1.

    CP2 — The bug: nothing passes the filter 🐛

    1. Execute the whole workflow. Everything is green, and #news stays empty. Nobody is notified of anything. A dead radar that reports "OK".
    2. Open the Filter (IF) node. It should let through articles whose title contains your keyword.
    3. Compare what the condition really says with what you want. Fix it.

    ✅ Done when: items come out of the filter's true branch. → CP2.

    Hint 1

    Look at the operator of the condition. "The title IS this word" and "the title CONTAINS this word" are very different.

    Hint 2

    A title is almost never one single word. is equal to will never match a full title against foodtech. Find the contains operator in the list.

    Hint 3 — think about it

    How do you catch this class of bug in production, when nobody is watching? One answer: an inverse alert — "I have sent nothing for 3 days, that is suspicious". Keep this idea. Your Séance 3 project needs a failure case, and this is a good one.

    CP3 — The alert goes out

    true branch → Slack node, message with title and URL. Execute.

    The skeleton's Slack node arrives set to #veille — change it to #news, like in Ex 1.1.

    ✅ Done when: alerts arrive, and only for relevant articles. → CP3.

    CP4 — Turn the radar on

    Schedule every 30 minutes → Publish.

    ✅ Done when: it is green. Your radar runs. → CP4. 🎉

    And the real problem with polling?

    Your radar sends you the same articles every 30 minutes. Annoying. That problem — "what is actually NEW?" — is exactly the subject of Ex 1.4. Want to look ahead? Open the Remove Duplicates node and read its option to remember state between runs.
